    Section 1 of 9

    Your status for tax purposes

    This first answer changes everything that follows. Immigration status and tax status are not the same thing, and getting this wrong is the hardest error to undo.

    Which describes you?

    If you are on an F-1 or J-1 visa and have been in the United States five calendar years or less, you are a nonresident alien for tax purposes even though you live here full time.

    Stop here. You file Form 1040-NR and Form 8843, not Form 1040. TurboTax and FreeTaxUSA do not support nonresident returns and will silently produce a wrong filing. Read the international students guide, then contact your international student office, which usually provides free Sprintax access.
    We will flag this in your plan rather than guessing. The substantial presence test counts all your days in the US this year, one third of last year's, and one sixth of the year before. Reaching 183 makes you a resident for tax purposes. F-1 and J-1 students do not count any days for their first five calendar years. If that describes you, read the international students guide before continuing.
    Do you have a Social Security number or ITIN?

    You need one to file. This tool never asks for the number itself.

    Section 3 of 9

    Can someone claim you

    You never claim yourself as a dependent. Your parents claim you, and you acknowledge it by checking a box on page one of Form 1040. These three questions decide whether that box gets checked.

    Did you pay more than half of your own support?

    Support means housing, food, tuition, medical care, transportation, and clothing for the whole year. Scholarships you received as a student do not count as support you provided. Student loans in your own name do count as your support.

    Where did you live for more than half the year?

    Time away at school counts as living at your parents' home. A dorm does not break this test.

    Have you confirmed with your parents whether they are claiming you?

    If you both claim, the second return filed gets rejected and has to go in on paper. One conversation prevents a two month delay.

      Have you or your parents claimed the American Opportunity Credit before?

      It has a four year lifetime limit per student. Years your parents claimed it for you count against your total.

      Were you enrolled at least half time in a degree program?

      Required for the American Opportunity Credit. The Lifetime Learning Credit has no enrollment minimum.

      Section 7 of 9

      Other situations

      These come up often enough for students that skipping them causes real problems.

      Did you have marketplace health insurance through Healthcare.gov or a state exchange?

      If yes you receive Form 1095-A and must file Form 8962 to reconcile the subsidy. Filing without it is the most common cause of a held return.

      You need Form 1095-A in hand before you file. It arrives by the end of January. Filing without reconciling on Form 8962 will hold your refund.
      Did you contribute to a retirement account?

      A Roth IRA or a 401k at a job. The Saver's Credit is worth up to $1,000, but full-time students and dependents are both disqualified from it.

      Did you have a foreign bank account or foreign income?

      Accounts abroad worth more than $10,000 at any point in the year require an FBAR filing, separate from your tax return and due in April.
