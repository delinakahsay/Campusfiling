Guide

Every form you might get, and where it hides

Almost nothing arrives in the mail anymore. Most of these sit in a portal you have to know to check.

Sections

  • Other

From an employer

  • ✓W-2One from every employer you had, including a summer job you left in August. Due to you by January 31. Usually posted in a payroll portal such as Workday, ADP, or Paylocity rather than mailed.
  • ✓1099-NECFrom clients or platforms that paid you as a contractor. Tutoring companies, freelance work, and research stipends often use this.
  • ✓1099-KFrom payment platforms such as PayPal, Venmo business accounts, Etsy, or delivery apps. Thresholds change often, and the income is taxable whether or not the form arrives.

From your school

  • ✓1098-TPosted to your student account or bursar portal in January. Box 1 is what was paid toward qualified tuition and fees. Box 5 is scholarships and grants applied. The difference between them determines whether scholarship money is taxable.
  • ✓Book and supply receiptsNot a tax form, but required course materials count toward the education credit and never appear on the 1098-T. Keep them.
  • ✓Scholarship award lettersUseful when the 1098-T does not make clear what a grant was designated for. Room and board designations make aid taxable.

From banks, brokerages, and apps

  • ✓1099-INTFrom any bank that paid you more than $10 in interest. High yield savings accounts generate these routinely now.
  • ✓1099-DIVFrom a brokerage if you held dividend paying funds or stocks.
  • ✓1099-BFrom a brokerage or crypto exchange for every sale, gain or loss. Robinhood and Coinbase post these in the app. These arrive later than W-2s, often mid-February, so do not file before checking.
  • ✓1098-EFrom a loan servicer if you paid $600 or more in student loan interest. Deductible up to $2,500, but only if you are not claimed as a dependent.

Other situations

  • ✓1095-AFrom Healthcare.gov or a state exchange if you had marketplace insurance. Required before you file, and it feeds Form 8962. Filing without it is a leading cause of held refunds.
  • ✓1099-GFrom a state unemployment office, or for a state tax refund you received in a prior year.
  • ✓1099-SA and 5498-SAFrom an HSA custodian if you had a health savings account, feeding Form 8889.
  • ✓Last year's returnFiling software asks for your prior year adjusted gross income to verify your identity when you e-file. Find it before you start, not halfway through.

If something never came

Check the portal first. Most missing forms are not missing, they are sitting behind a login the student stopped using after they left the job. Payroll portals stay accessible for a period after employment ends, and you can usually still reset the password.

If a W-2 truly never arrives, contact the employer. If they will not produce one, the IRS can be contacted after mid-February and you can file with Form 4852 as a substitute, using your final pay stub.

For anything from a school, the bursar's office rather than financial aid is who issues the 1098-T.
