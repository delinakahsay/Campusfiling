International students file a different return

If you are on an F-1 or J-1 visa, most of what you will read online about student taxes does not apply to you.

The short version

You file Form 1040-NR, not Form 1040. You must file Form 8843 even if you earned nothing at all. TurboTax and FreeTaxUSA cannot produce a nonresident return and will silently generate the wrong one.

Immigration status is not tax status

These are two separate systems that use similar words. You can be lawfully present, enrolled full time, and living in the United States year round while still being a nonresident alien for tax purposes.

The test is the substantial presence test, and F-1 and J-1 students are exempt from counting their days for the first five calendar years. During those years you are a nonresident for tax purposes regardless of how much time you actually spent here. J-1 scholars and researchers have a two year exempt period rather than five.

Once you pass that window, you generally begin filing like any other resident and the rest of this site applies to you normally.

Form 8843 is required even with zero income

This surprises almost everyone. Form 8843 is not a tax return. It is a statement explaining why your days of presence do not count toward the substantial presence test, and every exempt individual must file it annually.

If you had no US income at all, Form 8843 is the only thing you file. If you had income, it goes in with your Form 1040-NR. Missing years of Form 8843 can complicate a future change of status.

Why the usual software fails

TurboTax, FreeTaxUSA, and the IRS Free File partners support resident returns only. They will accept everything you type and produce a Form 1040, which is the wrong form for you.

The consequences are not immediate, which is what makes this dangerous. A wrongly filed 1040 can claim credits you were not entitled to, most commonly education credits and the standard deduction, and that surfaces years later during an immigration filing.

Use Sprintax or a comparable nonresident product. Most universities buy access codes and give them to students at no cost through the international services office. Ask before you pay for anything.

Treaty benefits

The United States has income tax treaties with more than sixty countries, and many contain a student article that exempts a set amount of wages or scholarship income from tax. India's treaty additionally allows certain students the standard deduction, which is unusual.

Treaty benefits are claimed with Form 8233 given to your employer before you are paid, and reported on the return itself. A nonresident tax product will ask you which country you are from and apply the right article. General software will not.

If you already filed the wrong form

This is fixable and it is better to fix it than to hope. You file Form 1040-X to amend, attaching the correct Form 1040-NR. If you claimed credits you were not eligible for, you repay them.

Doing this voluntarily is straightforward. Having it discovered later, during a green card or visa renewal process, is not. Your international student office has seen this many times and can point you to help.
