Do I even file

I only made a few thousand dollars. Do I have to file?

Probably not required, but you should anyway. If your employer withheld federal tax from your paychecks, that money stays with the IRS unless you file a return asking for it. For most students with a campus job, the entire amount comes back.

Filing becomes required once your earned income passes your standard deduction, your unearned income passes $1,350, or you had $400 or more of net self-employment profit.

My parents claim me. Does that mean I do not file?

No. Those are two separate returns. Your parents claiming you affects the size of your standard deduction and who gets the education credit. It has nothing to do with whether you file.

You file your own return, check the box acknowledging that someone can claim you, and take the reduced dependent standard deduction.

I had no income at all. Is there any reason to file?

Usually not for federal purposes. Two exceptions: if you are an international student on an F-1 or J-1 visa, you must file Form 8843 regardless of income. And if you had marketplace health insurance, you need to file to reconcile the subsidy.

Dependency

How do I know if my parents can claim me?

Three tests for the qualifying child rule. You were under 19, or under 24 and a full-time student for at least five months. You did not pay more than half your own support. And you lived with them more than half the year, where time away at school still counts as living at home.

If you are too old for that rule, a second one still applies. Under the qualifying relative test you can be claimed at any age if your gross income is under $5,350 and someone else provides more than half your support. Grad students often land here.

Meeting all three means they can claim you, whether or not they choose to. The reduced standard deduction applies either way.

Can I just say nobody claims me so I get the bigger deduction?

No. The test is whether you can be claimed, not whether anyone actually did. A parent declining to claim you does not give you the full standard deduction. This is a common piece of bad advice and it produces an incorrect return.

My scholarship covers most of my costs. Does that count as me supporting myself?

No. Scholarships received by a student are excluded from the support calculation entirely. They count as neither your support nor your parents'. Student loans in your own name do count as support you provided.

Scholarships and credits

Is my scholarship taxable?

The part that exceeds tuition, required fees, and required books and supplies is. Aid that pays for room, board, travel, or optional equipment is taxable income to you.

It goes on Schedule 1, line 8r, listed as scholarship and fellowship grants not reported on Form W-2, and flows to line 8 of your Form 1040. Advice telling you to write SCH beside the wages line is out of date, that changed for tax year 2022. Software will not find this on its own from your 1098-T, and nothing was withheld from it, which is why some students who expected a refund end up owing.

Who claims the education credit, me or my parents?

Whoever claims you as a dependent claims the credit. If your parents claim you, the 1098-T goes on their return even though it has your name on it. Send them the form and your book receipts.

If you claim it by mistake while they also claim it, both returns get flagged and at least one has to be amended.

I file independently. Can I claim the American Opportunity Credit?

Yes, on Form 8863, worth up to $2,500 with a four year lifetime limit per student. Years your parents claimed it for you count against that limit. Once those four years are used, the credit is gone for you permanently and the Lifetime Learning Credit is the fallback.

One catch. If you are under 24 and have a living parent, the refundable portion is disallowed unless you provided more than half your own support from earned income. The credit offsets tax you owe but will not generate a refund on its own.

Gig and side income

I drove for DoorDash. What is different?

You are self-employed for tax purposes. Once net profit reaches $400 you file Schedule C to report the business and Schedule SE to calculate self-employment tax, which runs 15.3 percent of net profit.

Nobody withheld that for you, so it is owed at filing. Deduct your expenses first: mileage at the standard rate, the business share of your phone bill, platform commissions, and supplies all come off gross income before the tax is calculated.

I got paid through Venmo and never received a form. Do I report it?

Yes. The income is taxable whether or not a form arrives. Platform reporting thresholds change frequently and a missing form is not an exemption.

I sold some stock or crypto. Does that matter if I lost money?

Yes. Every sale is reportable, gain or loss. Losses can offset gains and up to $3,000 of ordinary income, so reporting a loss often helps you. Robinhood and Coinbase post these forms in the app rather than mailing them.

States

I go to school in another state. Am I a resident there now?

Almost always no. Attending school somewhere does not change your domicile. Your driver's license, voter registration, and permanent address determine it, and those usually stay with your parents' home.

I worked at my school in a different state. Do I file twice?

Sometimes, and the order matters when it applies. Check two things first. Some jurisdictions do not tax nonresident wages at all, DC being the big one for students in the Washington area, so there is no nonresident return to file. Others have reciprocity agreements, such as Maryland, Virginia, Pennsylvania, and West Virginia with one another. When neither applies, file the nonresident return for the state where you worked first, then your home state resident return, claiming a credit for tax paid to the other state.

Doing it in the reverse order means the credit will not calculate and you will be taxed twice on the same wages.

What if my state has no income tax?

Then there is no return to file there. Nine states do not tax wage income: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming.

Software and cost

Do I have to pay to file?

No. IRS Free File partners cost nothing for federal returns at student income levels, and Free File Fillable Forms are open to everyone regardless of income. FreeTaxUSA is free for federal including self-employment forms, with a modest per state charge.

What about TurboTax?

It works, but be ready for the free tier to end. Adding a 1098-T, a 1099, or a state return typically moves you to a paid product. Intuit settled with the Federal Trade Commission over how that free edition was advertised.

If you would rather not pay, FreeTaxUSA handles the same forms at no federal cost.

How long until I get my refund?

Roughly three weeks with e-file and direct deposit. Paper returns and mailed checks take considerably longer, and a check sent to a campus address over the summer is a common way for refunds to go missing. Choose direct deposit.

Mistakes and late filing

I already filed and I think I made a mistake.

You amend with Form 1040-X. Wait until the original return has been processed, then file the amendment. There is no penalty for correcting something yourself, and amendments can be e-filed now rather than mailed.

I did not file last year, or the year before.

You can still file. Refunds expire three years after the original deadline, so there is a real window and it does close. Each year needs that year's forms and that year's software.

If you were owed a refund, there is no penalty for filing late. The penalties apply when you owed money.

I owe money and cannot pay it.

File on time anyway. The failure to file penalty is roughly ten times the failure to pay penalty, so filing without payment is far better than not filing. The IRS offers payment plans you can set up online.
